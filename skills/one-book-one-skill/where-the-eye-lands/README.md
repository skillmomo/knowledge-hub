# Where the Eye Lands (落点)

A composition skill for AI. *One Book, One Skill (一书一 skill) · 03*

Author: skill整理员

## What it does

> "What your eye sees is, in essence, a response to contrast. The stronger the contrast, the more it draws your attention." (Ian Roberts)

By default, AI makes everything equally clear, bright, and large, so the eye has nowhere to land. Where the Eye Lands makes the AI plan the picture before drawing anything: it sets a path for the viewer's eye.

- **Frame**: choose the format and proportions first; the four edges are the most important lines.
- **Squint**: reduce the picture to three or four big masses of light and dark, each a different size.
- **Skeleton**: one main direction (diagonal, triangle, S-curve, ...) that links the big masses.
- **Edges**: contrast lives at the edges between masses; give hard edges to the focus and soften the rest.
- **Focal point**: exactly one place gets the strongest contrast; everything else steps back.
- **Step back**: check at thumbnail size and mirrored; remove anything that pulls the eye off its path.

It includes six knowledge chapters, a five-layer working order (movement, structure, big shapes, subject, details), a five-step "focal point" check, and guidance for posters and covers, slides and web pages, photo cropping, AI image prompts, video shots, interior views, and page layout.

## When to use it

When an image, slide, cover, or page looks busy or has no clear focus. Typical requests mention "composition", "layout", "focal point", "visual hierarchy", "cropping", or "Where the Eye Lands".

## How to use

Copy the `where-the-eye-lands` folder into your skills directory (for Claude Code: `~/.claude/skills/`). The skill file (`SKILL.md`) is written in Chinese.

## Source

Based on reading notes from Ian Roberts, *Mastering Composition: Techniques and Principles to Dramatically Improve Your Painting* (2008), Chinese edition 《构图的艺术》 (Shanghai People's Fine Arts Publishing House, 2012). The skill is an original summary with short, cited quotations. Its uses beyond painting (slides, rooms, writing) are extensions, not the author's words. The contrast maps used in testing are computed, not eye-tracking studies.
